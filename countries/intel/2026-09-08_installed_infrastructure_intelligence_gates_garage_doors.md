## The Failure Ontology

### Motor Failures (by frequency)
1. Capacitor failure (3-8 years, EUR 15-40)
2. Gearbox failure (5-10 years, EUR 100-300)
3. Motor burnout (8-15 years, EUR 200-600+)
4. Encoder/sensor failure (3-7 years, EUR 50-150)
5. Hydraulic seal failure (5-10 years, EUR 100-300)

### PCB Failures
1. Power surge damage (any age, EUR 150-300+)
2. Moisture/corrosion (3-10 years, board replacement)
3. Relay welding (5-10 years, EUR 80-200)
4. Solder joint failure (7-12 years, EUR 50-150)

### Remote Control Issues
1. Dead/weak battery (#1 cause, EUR 2-5)
2. Rolling code out of sync (free reprogramming)
3. RF interference 433MHz (EUR 10-30)
4. Receiver board failure (EUR 80-200+)

### Safety/Regulatory
- EN 13241 mandatory for CE marking
- EN 12453 safety requirements
- Machinery Directive applies to motorised gates
- UK HSE actively investigates gate incidents
- 10-year conformity document retention required

## Repair vs Replacement Economics

| Motor Age | Recommendation | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| <4 years | Always repair | Installation error or defect |
| 4-8 years | Evaluate | Depends on fault and brand |
| 8-12 years | Repair only if isolated + premium | Multiple degradation pathways |
| >12 years | Almost always replace | Beyond design life |

**Break-even:** ~35-40% of replacement cost (not 50%).

**Example:** Zappi PCB repair £169 vs £600+ replacement. That's 28% — well below break-even, so repair wins.

## New Pattern Discovered

> **The 35-40% repair/replacement break-even rule.** Repair wins when cost is <35-40% of replacement installed. This is lower than the commonly assumed 50% because fixed call-out costs eat into headroom. This means more repairs are viable than operators think, creating a larger service-lead market.
