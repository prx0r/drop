# Gold Registry

*Collated insights, generalized principles, and reusable patterns from all research.*
*These are the foundation for spawning new hypotheses. Every insight here has evidence behind it.*
*Updated: 2026-09-07*

---

## 1. The Maintenance Graph Insight

> **Do not chase the rapidly growing product. Chase the maintenance graph created by the installed base.**

The installation wave is visible and competitive. The 5-15 years of failures, replacements, compatibility problems, upgrades, consumables and local service searches behind it are much less efficiently served.

**Evidence:** Swedish heat-pump specialist has 46 SKUs for one old IVT 490 model alone. Norwegian EV discussions now focus on 6-year-old Easee charger failures.

---

## 2. The Good Seller Gap

> **Few sellers is not the same as few GOOD sellers. A market with 8 sellers and 1 good specialist is more interesting than a market with 2 sellers and 2 good specialists.**

**Evidence:** Finnish robot vacuums have 22 sellers but many are weak. RIDGID had "1 Finnish seller" publicly but deep B2B channels hidden behind login. Public-web sparsity is increasingly dangerous.

---

## 3. The Maintenance Graph Arbitrage Formula

> **Expensive installed asset + rich owner + horrible compatibility/problem search + fragmented local trades = extremely good middleman economics.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian EV charger owners discussing 6-year-old Easee failures. Finnish cabin water damage exceeding 10,000 cases in 2026. UK Zappi PCB repair at £169 vs £600+ replacement.

---

## 4. The CVR Reality

> **0.48% is realistic for high-ticket products. Most operators overestimate CVR by 10x. At 0.48% CVR, you need 536 clicks for 80% chance of 1 sale.**

**Evidence:** Pitiful_Gene $10k/day case had 0.48% CVR, not 4-5%. 7,500 sessions for 36 orders. $389 AOV.

**Implication:** $5/day budget gives ~15 clicks/day. That's 1 sale every 36 days at 0.48% CVR. Free listings first, then paid.

---

## 5. The Localized Transaction Quality Thesis

> **For otherwise similar merchants, matching a country's preferred checkout, payment, delivery, returns, language and trust conventions increases profit per qualified visitor more than cosmetic presentation.**

**Evidence:** PostNord Spring 2026: 78% Norwegians buy cross-border. Vipps dominant. 60% abandoned checkout in 3 months. Shipping cost is #1 abandonment reason. Finland: parcel lockers most-used, 63% paid for delivery.

---

## 6. The Geographic Arbitrage Formula

> **Same product × different country × different competition × AI unlocks language barriers = exploitable edge.**

**Evidence:** PostNord: 86% Nordic consumers bought online within 30 days, 71% cross-border. Sweden becoming leading foreign origin for Norway. Finland dependent on international retailers.

---

## 7. The Installed Base > Trending Products

> **A product with 2 million installed units and 1/3 replacement rate is more interesting than a product trending on TikTok. Installed base creates recurring demand. Trends create one-time spikes.**

**Evidence:** Finland heat pumps: 2M installed, 1/3 replacements. Sweden: 46 SKUs for one old model. Norwegian EVs: 945k vehicles creating replacement tail.

---

## 8. The Source Market Oracle

> **Sweden is not where you launch stores. Sweden is where you discover what to transplant. 60% of Swedish ecommerce sells internationally. Find what works in Sweden, check if Finland/Norway has it, launch there.**

**Evidence:** PostNord: Swedish ecommerce companies list Norway, Denmark, Finland as top targets. Adapting checkout/payment is leading success factor.

---

## 9. The Specialist Trust Advantage

> **A store that positions as "X specialist" converts better than a store that sells "X + Y + Z". Narrow expertise creates trust. Generic breadth creates confusion.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian Davis weather stations: 8 retailers, none specialists. Specialist comparison pages expected to outperform marine store listings.

---

## 10. The Free-First Validation Principle

> **Never spend money on ads until free surfaces prove demand. Free listings, SEO content, and Pinterest generate signal at $0 cost. The cheapest possible market validation.**

**Evidence:** Every successful case in corpus started with free validation. Google free listings available in Norway/Finland. SEO content ranks in 2-4 weeks.

---

## 11. The Replication Compounds Principle

> **Each successful store generates reusable insights that make the next store faster and cheaper to launch. The asset is the system, not the stores.**

**Evidence:** Arthur & Bryan: 20 stores, $20M revenue, same playbook replicated. Templates, supplier relationships, and content libraries transfer between stores.

---

## 12. The Operations Bottleneck

> **After validation, the constraint moves from ads to operations. Supplier reliability, fulfillment speed, payment processing, customer support determine survival.**

**Evidence:** Pitiful_Gene: "Payment gateways, fulfillment, support become constraints after product validates." Shopify Payments switch → 46% sales drop.

---

## 13. The Pain-Attached Digital Product

> **Sell cheap certainty to people spending big. A $29 guide for a $1,000 decision has 95%+ margin and high perceived value. But test the UTILITY first, not the PRICE.**

**Evidence:** People spend NOK 15,000-40,000 on weather stations without buying guide. No Norwegian retailer offers expert content. AI generates guides at $0 marginal cost.

---

## 14. The Cross-Border Tax Regime

> **Three distinct regimes, not one "EU cross-border" field. Intra-EU (OSS), Import low-value (IOSS + €3 duty since Jul 2026), Import high-value (full customs). Norway: VOEC for items <NOK 3,000, standard for ≥NOK 3,000.**

**Evidence:** VOEC only simplifies for goods below NOK 3,000/item. EU abolished €150 duty exemption July 2026. Our products (NOK 15k-40k) are in standard regime.

---

## 15. The Bayesian Prior Must Be Realistic

> **Beta(1,1) with mean 50% CVR is catastrophically wrong for ecommerce rare events. After 100 clicks and 0 orders, it gives 73.8% probability of profitability. Use hierarchical empirical priors centered on realistic values (0.3-0.5% for high-ticket technical).**

**Evidence:** Direct comparison: Old Beta(1,1) = 73.8% PROMISING. New Beta(0.5,150) = 22.1% uncertain. 536 clicks needed for 80% chance of 1 sale at 0.3% CVR.

---

## 16. The Evidence Hierarchy

> **Not all evidence is equal. Distinguish: F0 (observational), F1 (free listings), F2 (paid traffic), F3 (transactions), F4 (fulfilled), F5 (replicated). Free listings are F1 evidence, not binary gates.**

**Evidence:** Google explicitly says free listings don't guarantee exposure. Zero impressions ≠ zero demand.

---

## 17. The Quality Over Quantity Rule

> **For merchants, quality matters more than quantity. A market with 8 sellers and 1 good specialist is more interesting than 2 sellers with 2 good specialists. Score merchants on 16 dimensions, not just count them.**

**Evidence:** Good Seller Gap model shows Finnish heat pump market: 12 sellers, only 2 good, gap score 76.1.

---

## 18. The Model-Specific Compatibility Play

> **Users search for their exact model number + problem. "Mitsubishi LN25 filter" converts better than "heat pump accessories". Build the compatibility graph, not the product catalog.**

**Evidence:** Swedish heat-pump specialist has 46 SKUs for one old IVT 490 model. Norwegian EV owners discuss specific Easee/Zaptec model failures.

---

## 19. The Geographic Targeting Done Right

> **Don't produce /city-name pages with the city word changed (doorway abuse). Make each page genuinely useful: local faults, partner availability, diagnosis process, local prices, reviews, regulations.**

**Evidence:** Google Search considers near-identical city pages as spam. But pages with real local content (partner availability, postcode-specific service, local regulations) are valuable.

---

## 20. The Temporal Arbitrage Engine

> **What a mature market searches for today is what a growing market will search for in 2-3 years. Use Sweden/Germany as leading indicators for Norway/Finland/Denmark.**

**Evidence:** Denmark EVs following Norway's curve several years later. Norwegian EV charger discussions about 6-year-old replacements will become Danish discussions by 2029.

---

## 21. The Multi-Monetization Stack

> **The same customer can be monetized four ways: parts → consumables → service leads → eventual replacement leads. Each layer has different economics and different competitive dynamics.**

**Evidence:** Swedish heat-pump specialist: filters, cleaning chemicals, remote controls, condensate drainage, heating cable, pipe covers, vibration dampers, covers and roofs. All from one installed base.

---

## 22. The Installed Base Lifecycle Model

```
PURCHASE → INSTALL → CONFIGURE → INTEGRATE → MAINTAIN →
CONSUMABLES → FAULT → DIAGNOSE → REPAIR → PARTS →
UPGRADE → REPLACE → REMOVE
```

**The mistake:** optimizing for PURCHASE + INSTALL.
**The opportunity:** everything BELOW them.

---

## 23. The Local Labour Advantage

> **Expensive local labour makes DIY and self-service more valuable. In Norway/Switzerland, where tradespeople cost $100-200/hour, a $50 diagnostic tool or compatibility guide has outsized value.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian cabin water damage exceeding 10,000 cases. Property owners can't be there. Remote diagnosis and self-service tools solve real problems.

---

## 24. The Safety/Regulatory Complexity Bonus

> **Products requiring electrical certification, building permits, or safety compliance create natural barriers to entry and increase the value of expert guidance.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian DSB publishes 4,113 registered electrical businesses. EV charger installation requires certified electricians. Heat pumps require professional installation.

---

## 25. The Recurring Consumable Pattern

> **Every installed base creates recurring demand for consumables: filters, cleaning supplies, replacement parts, batteries. These have 90%+ margins and predictable demand.**

**Evidence:** Swedish heat-pump specialist sells filters, cleaning chemicals, remote controls as recurring revenue. Finnish sauna specialist has 319 spare parts.

---

## 26. The Model Number as Search Intent

> **Users searching for their exact model number have the highest purchase intent. "Mitsubishi LN25 filter" converts better than "heat pump filter". Build the model-specific compatibility graph.**

**Evidence:** Swedish heat-pump specialist organizes by model. Norwegian EV discussions reference specific Easee/Zaptec model numbers and serial numbers.

---

## 27. The Fragmented Local Trades Opportunity

> **When local trades are fragmented (many small operators, no dominant player), a marketplace that aggregates and vets them creates value. Government registries provide the supply-side database.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian DSB publishes 4,113 registered electrical businesses with phone/email/Excel download. This is a ready-made supply-side marketplace database.

---

## 28. The Downtime Cost Multiplier

> **Products where downtime is expensive (heating in winter, EV charging for daily commuters, water systems in cabins) create urgency that increases willingness to pay for fast, reliable service.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian cabin water damage: 10,000+ cases in 2026, small leaks become structural damage. EV charger failures affect daily commuting.

---

## 29. The Hardware Aging Curve

> **Every hardware ecosystem has a predictable aging curve. 0-3 years: warranty claims. 3-6 years: first failures. 6-10 years: replacement parts, compatibility issues. 10+ years: full replacement. Identify where each ecosystem sits on this curve.**

**Evidence:** Norwegian Easee owners discussing 6-year-old charger failures. Swedish heat-pump specialist selling parts for old IVT 490 models. Finnish heat pumps entering replacement phase.

---

## 30. The Bundle Architecture

> **Don't sell a single product. Build a bundle around a lifecycle moment: diagnosis + parts + installation + maintenance + eventual replacement. The bundle is worth more than the sum of parts.**

**Evidence:** Swedish heat-pump specialist: 46 SKUs for one model (filter + remote + condensate + vibration + cover + cleaning + sensors + service). Each layer monetizes differently.

---

## 31. The Installed Infrastructure Intelligence Thesis

> **Owner-operated infrastructure + technical ambiguity + regulatory responsibility + fragmented specialists + expensive consequences of getting it error = an excellent diagnostic-commerce / lead-routing market.**

Eight independent forces: large installed base, owner responsibility, ageing infrastructure, diagnostic ambiguity, model complexity, fragmented trades, regulatory complexity, costly failure.

**Evidence:** Independently validated across EV charging, heat pumps, cabins, private wells, wastewater.

---

## 32. The Forcing-Function Score

> **Some searches are optional. Some are forced. Forced demand is beautiful — customers don't comparison-shop for three weeks while sewage comes out of the lawn.**

$$F = Regulatory + Safety + Failure + Financial + Seasonal$$

High F scores: failed government inspection (5), no water from well (5), sewage backing up (5).

## 33. The Knowledge Gap Score

> **High KG sectors are excellent middleman markets. Consumer may not know brand, tank location, pump type, blower function, desludging need, which part failed.**

$$KG = Complexity_{system} - Knowledge_{consumer}$$

## 34. The Mature-Market Oracle Pattern

> **Don't invent the target-country search universe. Mine Germany/UK/US for the mature aftermarket SKUs. Translate into installed-base hypotheses. Then test search demand.**

**Evidence:** German wastewater specialist has 225 accessories, 49 blowers for one model. Swedish heat-pump specialist has 46 SKUs for one old model.

---

## 35. The Four Monetization Classes

Every opportunity should be classified:

- **A — Ecommerce:** filters, bulbs, parts, consumables
- **B — Qualified service lead:** repair, installation, desludging
- **C — High-ticket replacement lead:** new system, new well
- **D — Recurring relationship:** annual service, reminders, subscriptions

Best ecosystems support all four simultaneously.

---

## 36. The Government Grant Multiplier

> **Grant eligibility turns expensive jobs into viable leads. Customer doesn't pay full price — government subsidizes. This changes unit economics dramatically.**

**Evidence:** Ireland septic grants up to €12,000. Well grants up to €5,000. 460 grants worth €4.77M in 2025 alone.

---

## 37. The 35-40% Repair/Break-Even Rule

> **Repair wins when cost is <35-40% of replacement installed. This is lower than the commonly assumed 50% because fixed call-out costs eat into headroom.**

**Evidence:** UK gate specialist guidance. Zappi PCB repair £169 vs £600+ replacement = 28% — well below break-even, so repair wins.

---

## 38. The Regulatory Complexity as Moat

> **Safety standards (EN 13241, Machinery Directive) create compliance barriers that favor established brands and trained installers. This increases the value of intermediaries who translate regulatory requirements.**

**Evidence:** UK HSE actively investigates gate incidents. 10-year conformity document retention required. Installer bears legal responsibility for CE conformity.

---

## 39. The Public Dataset Advantage

> **Official statistics (installed base, inspection failures, grant activity, well locations) provide the supply-side intelligence layer. No scraping needed — government data is better.**

**Evidence:** Ireland CSO: 498,283 systems in CSV/JSON-stat/PX. EPA: inspection data. Geological Survey: well locations.
