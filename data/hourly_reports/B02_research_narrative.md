# GoldProbe B02 — Full research narrative + structured JSON

GoldProbe batch B02 is complete. I kept it deliberately comparative: five cells, including two controls/negative findings, rather than generating hundreds of speculative opportunities.

CURRENT RANKING
1. Ireland — Stairlifts / home mobility — 9.4/10: excellent circular economics + urgency + ageing population.
2. Finland — Legacy ventilation/HRV replacement — 9.2/10: huge compatibility/physical-interface problem at replacement age.
3. New Zealand — Pool compliance + equipment passport — 9.0/10: regulation creates deterministic reacquisition; assets heavily concentrated among wealthy owners.
4. Norway — Generic ventilation filters — 6.7/10 CONTROL: installed base attractive, but consumable commerce already mature.
5. Ireland — Automatic gates — 6.2/10 HOLD: attractive unit economics, but insufficient installed-base denominator.

The controls are important. GoldProbe needs to become good at saying: “this looks attractive but the evidence does not justify entry.”

1. FINLAND VENTILATION — THE BETTER OPPORTUNITY IS REPLACEMENT, NOT FILTERS

The filter market initially looks perfect: recurring purchase, model compatibility, homeowners, cold climate, important equipment. But Sweden shows what happens once commerce catches up.

At one Swedish specialist I matched nine exact Flexit OEM/compatible filter pairs. The original branded filter commanded a median 94.4% higher list price than the compatible alternative. Examples: UNI 2: 424 SEK compatible -> 824 SEK OEM; UNI 3: 347 -> 653; UNI 4: 386 -> 776; Spirit K2 R: 330 -> 877; S4/S6/S7R: 423 -> 1,067. Observed OEM premiums ranged approximately 67%-166%.

Important: this is not yet revealed willingness-to-pay. We do not have unit sales. It is a live price ladder showing that retailers believe the market supports a substantial certainty/original-part premium.

That is our first proper GoldProbe measurement of: how much extra is being charged for perceived certainty even after compatibility has been solved?

But generic filters are not the opportunity. Norway already has model-based filter stores and subscription systems. That is evidence that the ecosystem is healthy, but also that we would be late attacking the obvious consumable.

The interesting transition happens around 20-25 years. Vallox explicitly gives technical ventilation equipment roughly that service life. It describes replacing a 1990s Finnish unit where old heat recovery was around 40% versus as much as 80% for modern equipment. More importantly, the successor unit was specifically engineered to match old duct outlets so installation required little duct modification.

That creates a different GoldProbe cell: old Vallox model -> what modern machine physically replaces it?

Proposed product: Ventilation Replacement Passport. Upload equipment photo, nameplate, house year, duct configuration if known, postcode. Return current unit, likely age, known physical successors, duct compatibility, installation complexity, indicative energy-recovery improvement, local installers, and future filter/support availability. Monetize the replacement lead.

NEW ECONOMIC PATTERN #1 — LEGACY_INTERFACE_LOCK_IN
An embedded physical asset leaves behind holes, ducts, wiring, plumbing, mounting points, dimensions and control interfaces. Those become switching constraints decades later. Therefore value of compatibility advice rises as physical replacement complexity rises.

2. NORWAY VENTILATION — VALUABLE NEGATIVE

Norway's government is actively stimulating energy renovation. Since the new Enova programme opened in August 2025, balanced ventilation alone received 2,903 approved applications in roughly its first half-year. Current support pays 25% of eligible invoiced expenditure up to NOK 15,000, with technical and documentation constraints.

Strong demand, but do not sell filters. The ecosystem has already matured there.

The better product is a grant-aware upgrade/replacement router: should I upgrade, do I qualify, what equipment fits, which installer, is this quote correctly specified, does it meet Enova requirements, where is the balancing documentation? The intermediary job is not a directory; it is translating the house into a correctly specified job so three quotes are genuinely comparable.

3. NEW ZEALAND POOLS — TWO EXTRAORDINARY PATTERNS

NZ has an estimated up to 100,000 residential pools, but distribution is dramatically skewed by wealth. Properties sold under NZ$1m: 6% have pools; NZ$1m-3m: 15%; above NZ$3m: 37%; Auckland houses above NZ$7.5m: 73%. Thus the >NZ$3m segment has about 6.17x the pool incidence of sub-NZ$1m homes.

NEW PATTERN #2 — LUXURY_DENSITY_OVER_POPULATION_DENSITY
For discretionary installed assets, service TAM can concentrate far more sharply by property value than by population. Geographic acquisition should weight asset density × owner value × failure spend, not raw household count.

Even better: applicable residential pool barriers must be inspected every three years. Failure can lead to reinspection, remediation and enforcement. Current council examples: New Plymouth NZ$270 inspection and NZ$246 reinspection; Thames-Coromandel NZ$250; Lower Hutt NZ$250/hour audit.

NEW PATTERN #3 — COMPLIANCE_CLOCK_REACQUISITION
A statutory recurring inspection date creates a predictable future commercial event before failure. This enables pre-inspection diagnosis, reminders, remediation routing and recurring acquisition.

Potential product: PoolPassport.nz. Store barrier type, next inspection, previous report, pump, chlorinator, heater, filter, robot, lights, warranties and service history. Trigger reminders and pre-inspection checks before the compliance date.

Australia becomes the mature-market oracle because its pool/spa installed base is much larger and its specialist aftermarket is deeper.

4. IRELAND STAIRLIFTS — BEST SURPRISE

Ireland now has 891,100 people aged 65+, 16.1% of the population, up 165,000 since 2020 (+22.7%). Current Irish offers include refurbished straight lifts from about EUR1,200-1,300, refurbished curved lifts from about EUR4,500, and all-inclusive leasing from EUR85/month.

What matters is the lifecycle. Operators buy unwanted stairlifts back, rebuild them, resell them, lease them, service them, replace batteries, eventually buy them back again and strip obsolete machines for parts.

NEW PATTERN #4 — CIRCULAR_ASSET_LIQUIDITY
When human need duration is shorter than engineering asset life, exit events become supply. The same physical unit can generate installation revenue, recurring lease/rental, maintenance, battery replacement, removal, buy-back spread, second installation and eventual parts revenue.

Urgency also matters. A family may search because a parent is being discharged from hospital in days. The actual decision is buy vs refurbished vs lease vs short rent vs grant, plus installation speed. The intermediary product is therefore a neutral urgent decision router, not a webshop.

5. NEW PATTERN #5 — OEM_CERTAINTY_PRICE_LADDER

The Swedish ventilation data suggests that even when two products satisfy the same compatibility requirement, the market maintains enormous price separation around original/known-correct/trusted. Nine matched pairs produced minimum OEM premium 66.6%, median 94.4%, mean 108.4%, maximum 165.8%.

This is not yet proven realized willingness-to-pay. Next tests should look at review counts, bestseller signals, stock-turn proxies, shopping prominence and merchant price dispersion.

6. AUTOMATIC GATES — USEFUL FAILURE

Automatic gates superficially fit well: compatibility is messy, safety matters, and live Irish spare/component prices span cheap remotes through controllers, motors and intercoms. But I could not obtain a credible Irish residential installed-base denominator.

GoldProbe therefore downgrades it.

METHODOLOGICAL RULE — DATA_OPACITY_PENALTY
No installed-base denominator -> lower confidence. No credible demand measure -> lower confidence. No price measure -> lower confidence. No geographic distribution -> do not pretend we know where to launch.

EMERGING COMPARISON
We are now distinguishing specific reasons customers pay rather than putting everything into generic “convenience”:
- Certainty premium: Swedish OEM filter price ladder.
- Physical compatibility value: Finnish legacy ventilation constrained by existing ducts.
- Compliance avoidance: NZ mandatory three-year pool inspections.
- Failure/remediation avoidance: inspection + reinspection + enforcement.
- Wealth concentration: NZ pool prevalence 37% at >NZ$3m homes vs 6% under NZ$1m.
- Time urgency: Irish stairlifts can be fitted within days.
- Risk transfer: monthly stairlift lease bundles repairs/service/batteries/warranty.
- Temporary-need economics: stairlift buy-back/reuse.
- Subsidy/qualification complexity: Norway ventilation.
- Commodity saturation: Norway generic filters already mature.
- Data uncertainty: Irish gates downgraded despite attractive qualitative economics.

COUNTRY-LEVEL LEARNING
Finland: mature ecommerce makes routine replacement parts less interesting; the gap moves toward which modern embedded asset correctly replaces an old one.
Norway: government-backed home-energy upgrading creates transaction volume, while straightforward products commercialize quickly; coordination complexity is more attractive than catalogue scarcity.
New Zealand: raw population undersells niches; wealthy-property concentration plus strong regulation creates tiny geographic markets with extreme asset density and purchasing power.
Ireland: fragmented service + government intervention + ageing/rural/homeowner infrastructure + less mature aggregation than UK/Germany repeatedly create intermediary opportunities; stairlifts add secondary-market circulation.

ENGINE IMPLICATION
Opportunity should not be a naked score. Each input should carry value, measurement_type (observed/proxy/inferred), source and confidence. GoldProbe should explicitly model installed base, trigger rate, spend, willingness-to-pay/price evidence, urgency, fragmentation, forcing functions, competition, CAC and capture probability.

NEXT RESEARCH LOGIC
The next probe should deliberately test a mechanism not yet measured. Leading candidates: motorised shutters/awnings in Switzerland/Spain (climate + motor/controller compatibility), pellet/wood stoves in Alpine/Nordic markets (seasonal forcing and emergency premium), and domestic/sump/sewage pumps (urgent failure + cheap component vs expensive callout). The next probe must record why it was chosen, what would falsify it, whether it answered the intended hypothesis, and why that result determines the following probe.
