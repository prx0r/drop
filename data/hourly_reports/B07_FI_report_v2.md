## Executive result

B07 successfully **replicates the cohort-crossing mechanism**.

Finland is unusually useful because the replacement market is not inferred from device ages alone. SULPU directly reports that **more than one-third of 2025 air-to-air heat-pump sales were replacements**, while Sweden — where adoption started earlier — is already around **50% replacement share**.

Finland sold 96,000 air-to-air heat pumps in 2025. “More than one-third” therefore implies a transparent lower bound of:

# **>32,000 replacement air-to-air heat pumps in 2025**

That is association-reported market data, not an official government transaction census, but it is an independently observed replacement wave.

Historic Finnish adoption and plausible lifetime assumptions line up with it:
- >500k heat pumps already existed by 2012 according to SULPU;
- 45,000 air-to-air pumps were sold in 2012;
- 53,000 air-to-air pumps were sold in 2014;
- Finnish modelling has used **15 years** as the baseline air-to-air lifetime assumption, with **10 years** as a sensitivity case.

The mechanism can now move from **CANDIDATE → REPLICATED**.

SULPU describes Finland as having **almost 2 million installed heat pumps** and explicitly lists the **start of the air-to-air replacement market** as one cause of the strong growth.

Source:
https://www.sulpu.fi/5132-2/

Important: SULPU also lists cold winter, weak comparison-year sales and improving economic conditions. We do **not** attribute +71% to replacement alone.

## 4. Lifetime evidence

A Finnish heat-pump/electrified-heating study hosted by SULPU contains a sensitivity analysis that uses:

- **15-year air-to-air heat-pump life** in the baseline;
- **10 years** as an alternative sensitivity case.

The model's estimated 2019 air-to-air stock falls from 770,000 to 530,000 if 10 years is assumed instead of 15.

Source:
https://www.sulpu.fi/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/SET-sa%CC%88hko%CC%88istyva%CC%88-la%CC%88mmitys-isbn9789526401638.pdf

This is **not measured survival data**. It establishes a plausible 10–15 year timing window.

Motiva separately says air-to-water pumps generally have a technical life of around **15 years**.

Source:
https://www.motiva.fi/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/09/Lampopumppujen-huolto-ohje.pdf

So equipment installed in the early 2010s is now squarely in a plausible replacement-age regime.

# Pattern promotion: COHORT_CROSSING_COMPONENT_LIFETIME

The reusable formulation is now:

> **When historical installation cohorts are observable and a critical durable asset/component has a shorter measurable life than the surrounding property/system, cohort ageing can forecast a market regime change from acquisition toward replacement aftermarket.**

Critical qualification:

> It predicts **when the market changes**, not how many units will fail.

Never do:

`historic cohort × guessed annual failure rate`

without a real survival curve.

## 8. The actual replacement problem is richer

A high-value old-system customer needs to decide:

### Repair?
Does a €150–€300 service solve it?

### Specific component repair?
Fan bearing?
PCB?
Remote?
Sensor?
Drainage?
Refrigerant issue?

### Replace?
If so, with what?

### Reuse the current location?
Old wall penetration?
Brackets?
Pipe path?
Condensate route?

### Which current machine is the right successor?
Cold-weather performance?
Noise?
Heating vs cooling objective?
Spare-parts availability in 5–10 years?

SULPU itself explicitly tells buyers to ensure **spare parts and maintenance remain available five and ten years later**.

Source:
https://www.sulpu.fi/lampopumput/lampopumpun-hankinta/

That is an unusually clear statement of lifecycle economics.

## 15. What B07 changes in GoldProbe

Historical installation data should now become a **standard probe input**, not optional context.

For every durable asset:

1. obtain annual installation/sales history;
2. obtain component/system life distribution if possible;
3. align cohorts with current date;
4. independently search for observed replacement share / service growth;
5. only then generate aftermarket hypothesis.

This can let us find markets **before** replacement keywords become obvious.
