# Quality.md — The Standard

*This is the quality bar. Every response should meet or exceed this level.*
*Compare your work against this to see where you fall short.*

---

## What Good Looks Like

The field report above demonstrates:

1. **Primary sources cited** — Every claim references a specific Reddit thread, PostNord report, or government source
2. **Credibility grading** — Each piece of evidence is graded (High, Medium, Low, Very Low)
3. **Specific numbers** — Not "Scandinavia has good economics" but "86% shopped online in last 30 days, 78% cross-border"
4. **Counter-evidence included** — The strongest claims include the strongest counterevidence
5. **Mechanism identification** — Not just "this works" but "this works because X, Y, Z"
6. **Actionable conclusions** — Not vague advice but specific next steps
7. **Honest uncertainty** — "I don't know" is an acceptable answer
8. **Cross-referenced** — Claims verified against multiple sources

## What I Should Learn

1. **Cite sources** — Every claim needs a reference
2. **Grade evidence** — Not all claims are equal
3. **Include counter-evidence** — The strongest arguments include their weaknesses
4. **Identify mechanisms** — Not just "what" but "why"
5. **Be specific** — Numbers, not adjectives
6. **Be honest** — "I don't know" is acceptable
7. **Cross-reference** — Verify claims against multiple sources
8. **Be actionable** — Every section should lead to a decision

---

## The Quality Bar

Every response should answer:

1. **What do we know?** (with sources)
2. **How confident are we?** (credibility grading)
3. **What could invalidate this?** (counter-evidence)
4. **Why does this matter?** (mechanism)
5. **What should we do?** (actionable conclusion)
6. **What don't we know?** (honest uncertainty)

If a response doesn't answer all six, it's not ready.
